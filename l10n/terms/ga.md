# Irish (ga): term list

Machine translation, no native reviewer yet: the weakest of the seven. Irish is not a macOS interface language, and most Irish speakers use their Mac in English, so the plainest standard terms (An Caighdeán Oifigiúil, the wording of Microsoft's Irish software and focloir.ie / tearma.ie as far as I know them) are used, and widely used English technical words are kept (notch, shell, Prompter, Nook, AI).
Buttons and menu items are the imperative ("Oscail", "Dún", "Sábháil", "Cuir leis"); sentences speak to the user informally ("tú", "do"). Quotation marks “ ” (as in the English). No counted plural forms: "Gníomhairí: 3", "Oscailte: 3", "3 eile". Sentences are kept short and simple on purpose, to keep the mutations (séimhiú, urú) safe.

Apple app and Settings names: macOS shows them in English (there is no Irish macOS), so where a sentence sends the user to a macOS pane or app, the exact English name is kept: Privacy & Security, Full Disk Access, Screen Recording, Automation, Paste from Other Apps, Login Items, Contacts, Calendars, Notes, Messages, Music, Shortcuts, Activity Monitor, Weather.
Scout's own tab and setting names are translated and used the same everywhere (Socruithe › Ceist ar AI).
Finder, Spotlight, Keychain stay as they are.

Pixel-font keys (l10n/PIXEL-LABELS.txt): capitals, A-Z only, no fadas: ALL DAY = AN LA INTEIR, ASLEEP = AR CODLADH, BYE = SLAN, DONE = DEANTA, READY = REIDH, NEEDS YOU = TEASTAIONN TU, NO AGENTS = GAN GNIOMHAIRI, NO SIGNAL = GAN COMHARTHA, HELLO FROM SCOUT = DIA DHUIT O SCOUT, WELCOME = FAILTE.
Short widget titles prefer a word without fadas where one exists (Cuisle, Ceol, Aimsir, Tasc); where the natural word has one the pixel font draws it without the mark, which stays readable.
Brand names stay Latin: Scout, Claude, Claude Code, Codex, OpenAI, GPT, Anthropic, Mac, macOS, AirPods, AirDrop, Spotlight, Keychain, Zoom, Meet, Teams, Webex, Spotify. Digits 0-9. No dashes as separators (a colon or comma instead).
